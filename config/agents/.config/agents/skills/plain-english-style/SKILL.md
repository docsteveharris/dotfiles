# Plain English Style Skill

When this skill is active, rewrite text to meet Plain English standards without changing content or meaning. Use British English throughout.

## Core principles

Apply these techniques in order of priority:

### 1. Keep sentences short (average 15–20 words)

- One main idea per sentence, plus perhaps one related point
- Vary length: mix short punchy sentences with longer ones
- Break up long sentences at full stops; do not use commas to hide complexity

### 2. Use active verbs (aim for 80–90%)

- Structure: **subject** → **verb** → **object**
- Example (passive): "This matter will be considered by us shortly"
- Example (active): "We will consider this matter shortly"

**Good uses of passive** (exception to the 80–90% rule):
- To soften potentially hostile tone: "this has not been paid" (passive) vs "you have not paid this" (active)
- To avoid blame: "a mistake was made" (passive)
- When the doer is unknown: "the team has been picked"
- When it simply sounds better

### 3. Use 'you', 'we', 'I' (personalise)

- Address readers directly as 'you', not "the applicant" or "the customer"
- Refer to your organisation as 'we'
- Use 'I' for individual authors; 'we' for team efforts
- Example: "Applicants must send us…" → "You must send us…"

### 4. Use words appropriate for the reader

- Say exactly what you mean using the simplest words that fit
- Avoid jargon unless writing to specialists who use it
- Use everyday English whenever possible
- Do not assume lengthy words impress; they do not

### 5. Avoid nominalisations (abstract nouns derived from verbs)

Nominalisations make writing dull and heavy. Replace with the verb:

| ❌ Nominalisation | ✅ Verb |
|---|---|
| We had a discussion about the matter | We discussed the matter |
| There will be a stoppage of trains by drivers | Drivers will stop the trains |
| The implementation of the policy has been done by a team | A team has implemented the policy |

### 6. Give instructions directly (imperatives)

- Use commands: "Do this", "Please send it", "Split them where suitable"
- Avoid: "You should do this", "Customers should do this", "It is advised that…"
- Add "please" to soften if appropriate; omit "please" when action is mandatory

### 7. Use lists where appropriate

**For continuous sentences**, use semicolons and lower-case letters:

If you can prove that:
- you were somewhere else at the time;
- you were not related to Mary; and
- you are over 21;

then you should be all right.

**For separate points**, use bullet points with capitals and full stops:

Kevin needed to take:
- A penknife
- Some string
- A pad of paper
- A pen

Always use bullet points (not numbers or letters) unless they add necessary information.

### 8. Remove useless words

Delete words and phrases that add nothing:

| ❌ Useless | ✅ Remove or replace |
|---|---|
| at this moment in time | now |
| in the event of | if |
| in accordance with | under, keeping to |
| in excess of | more than |
| prior to | before |
| in respect of | for, about |
| the sum of | (delete) |
| each and every one | each one |

## Word alternatives

For a quick check, the most common replacements are:

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use |
|---|---|
| additional | extra |
| advise | tell |
| applicant | you |
| commence | start |
| comply with | keep to |
| ensure | make sure |
| forward | send |
| in the majority of instances | most, mostly |
| obligatory | must |
| per annum | a year |
| persons | people |
| purchase | buy |
| terminate | end |
| utilise | use |
| whilst | while |

**For the full list of 300+ words and phrases (A–Z), always check `references/a-to-z-alternative-words.md` before finalising an edit.** This includes entries such as notwithstanding, expeditiously, pursuant to, heretofore, and hundreds more bureaucratic terms with plain alternatives, plus a list of words and phrases to remove entirely (e.g. "at this moment in time", "in view of the fact that", "to all intents and purposes").

## Proofreading workflow

When editing text, apply changes in this sequence:

1. **Identify and replace nominalisations** → replace with verbs
2. **Convert passive verbs to active** (except where listed above)
3. **Shorten sentences** → split at full stops where needed
4. **Replace jargon and long words** → use Plain English alternatives
5. **Remove useless words** → delete or replace
6. **Personalise** → add 'you', 'we', 'I' where appropriate
7. **Check lists** → ensure correct punctuation and structure
8. **Read aloud** → check for naturalness and flow

## British English note

- Use British spellings: realise, organised, colour, centre
- Use British vocabulary and phrasing throughout

## Standing instructions

- Always preserve the original meaning and content
- Do not change technical accuracy or specific facts
- Flag any changes that risk altering tone or intent
- When in doubt, err on the side of clarity
- Reference the Plain English Campaign guides as the authoritative standard
